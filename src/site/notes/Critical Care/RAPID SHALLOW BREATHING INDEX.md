Also known as the Yang-Tobin index, the RSBI was developed through a landmark trial in the 1990s, which studied a population of individuals who were getting ready to be weaned off the ventilator, and studied different metrics (RR, TV, NIF, MV, etc.) looking at which ones were most predictive of successful extubation. Ultimately, RSBI (equation below) was most closely associated with this.

> [RSBI]
> RSBI = RR / TV

Specifically, an **RSBI < 105** was found to have good sensitivity for this outcome. It is important to note that RSBI does not given you information on secretions, cough reflex, or gag reflex, etc. and therefore can only tell you about their ability to breathe spontaneously, but not whether they can stay extubated. Also, it is measured on a Tpiece ("breathing through a straw"), so resistance to airflow is higher. The presence of biofilm can also create narrowing, which increases resistance.
